Angel City FC returned to NWSL competition in impressive fashion Friday night, defeating the Orlando Pride 2-0 at BMO Stadium. Playing its first match in more than a month, Angel City showed little rust as it controlled the early proceedings, created the better chances and earned a clean sheet against one of the league’s most dangerous attacking teams.

The victory marked a successful debut for interim head coach Leif Gunnar Smerud, who took charge after Angel City parted ways with Alexander Straus during the league break. Smerud’s team played with renewed energy and aggression, pressing Orlando high up the field while quickly moving the ball into threatening positions.

Angel City’s pressure produced the opening goal in the 36th minute. Ary Borges had an attempt blocked inside the penalty area, but the loose ball fell to Maiara Niehues on the right side of the box. Niehues fired a right-footed finish across goal and into the left side of the net to give the hosts a deserved 1-0 advantage.

The goal was Niehues’ fourth of the season, moving her into the team lead. It also rewarded an opening half in which Angel City outshot Orlando 10-2 and prevented the visitors from recording a shot on target. The Pride struggled to play through Angel City’s pressure and entered halftime without seriously testing goalkeeper Angelina Anderson.

Angel City doubled its lead in the 56th minute through Sveindís Jónsdóttir, who returned to the lineup after missing time before the break with an injury. Jónsdóttir dispossessed Orlando defender Rafaelle, accelerated into the right side of the penalty area and placed a low shot beyond goalkeeper Anna Moorhouse into the far corner.

Jónsdóttir’s goal highlighted the directness and athleticism that made Angel City difficult to contain throughout the night. Her ability to pressure defenders and attack open space gave Orlando problems, while the home side’s midfield consistently won second balls and disrupted the Pride before they could establish a rhythm.

Orlando increased its possession and pushed more players forward during the second half, with veteran midfielder Marta attempting to organize the attack. Angel City’s defense remained composed, however, limiting clear opportunities and protecting the two-goal advantage without allowing the match to become stretched.

The Pride’s best opportunity arrived in the 80th minute when Solai Washington broke into the left side of the box. Anderson reacted quickly, diving to deny Washington and preserve the shutout on Orlando’s only shot on target. The save completed a disciplined performance from an Angel City back line that absorbed pressure without surrendering control.

The result gave Angel City a measure of revenge after Orlando earned a dramatic 2-1 victory in the teams’ first meeting of the season in April. More importantly, the win provided a positive beginning to Smerud’s tenure and valuable momentum as Angel City entered the second half of its schedule with a more energetic and confident performance.
