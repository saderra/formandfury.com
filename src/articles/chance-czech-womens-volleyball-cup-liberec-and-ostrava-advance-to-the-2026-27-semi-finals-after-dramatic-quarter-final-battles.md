The Chance Czech Women's Volleyball Cup continued to deliver high-quality competition on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, as the quarter-final stage produced two important victories and confirmed additional teams for the semi-finals of the 2026/27 tournament. With a place among the final four at stake, Dukla Liberec and TJ Ostrava successfully navigated challenging encounters against Lvi Praha and Olymp Praha respectively. Both matches demonstrated the competitive character of Czech women's volleyball, with one encounter requiring a deciding fifth set and the other featuring a determined attempt at a comeback.



The standout contest of the evening took place between VK Lvi Praha and VK Dukla Liberec. The two teams produced an absorbing five-set encounter that lasted almost two and a half hours, with neither side willing to surrender its opportunity to continue in the national cup. Liberec eventually secured a 3–2 victory, winning by set scores of 25–20, 21–25, 25–15, 23–25 and 15–8. The result confirmed Dukla's progression into the semi-finals and maintained the team's impressive unbeaten run during the current season.



Dukla Liberec began the match confidently, establishing an early advantage through effective blocking and powerful serving. Katarina Körmendyová and Denisa Pavlíková played important roles in creating pressure from the service line, helping the visitors establish a six-point advantage during the opening set. Lvi Praha responded with determined attacking play, including contributions from Lucie Janská and Joséfina Smolková, but Liberec maintained control to take the opening set 25–20. The visitors' strong defensive organisation and ability to generate pressure at the net established an early indication of their intentions.



However, the Prague side demonstrated considerable resilience in the second set. Joséfina Smolková opened with an ace, while captain Simona Bajusz produced an impressive serving sequence that included three aces. Her contribution helped Lvi Praha transform a closely contested beginning into a commanding 13–6 advantage. The home team maintained its momentum and eventually levelled the match with a 25–21 set victory. The response demonstrated that Lvi Praha possessed the attacking quality and determination required to challenge one of the competition's strongest contenders.



Liberec regained control in the third set, using its serving strength and defensive discipline to disrupt the home team's rhythm. Carley Spachman contributed an important serving run as Dukla established an 8–3 advantage. The visitors continued to dictate the tempo, limiting Prague's opportunities to develop consistent attacking combinations. An ace from Aneta Kopejtková provided one of the few positive moments for the home supporters, but Liberec comfortably completed the set at 25–15 to move within one set of qualification.



The fourth set brought another determined response from Lvi Praha. Bajusz again influenced proceedings from the service line, helping the home team establish an 11–8 lead. Although Dukla repeatedly reduced the deficit and threatened to turn the set around, Prague maintained its composure during the closing stages. Important blocks from Marie Pomrová and Josefína Smolková helped the home side withstand the pressure, allowing Lvi Praha to claim the set 25–23 and force a decisive tiebreak. The outcome ensured that the semi-final qualification battle would be settled by a final race to 15 points.



Dukla Liberec delivered its strongest response when it mattered most. In the deciding set, the visitors demonstrated greater consistency and made effective use of their experience in high-pressure situations. Their defensive organisation and attacking efficiency prevented Lvi Praha from building another comeback, while Liberec steadily established control of the scoreboard. The visitors eventually secured a convincing 15–8 victory to complete the 3–2 result. For Dukla, the achievement represented another significant step in a strong campaign, while Lvi Praha were left to reflect on a competitive performance that came close to producing a major cup upset.



The second quarter-final of the evening featured TJ Ostrava and PVK Olymp Praha, with the home team eventually claiming a 3–1 victory. Ostrava started impressively, winning the opening two sets 25–15 and 25–11 to establish a commanding position. The home side appeared firmly in control, combining effective attacking play with a disciplined approach to defence. Olymp Praha, however, refused to accept an early elimination and responded after the short interval with an improved performance. The visitors captured the third set 25–22, introducing fresh uncertainty into the contest and briefly raising the possibility of another extended quarter-final battle.



Ostrava responded positively in the fourth set, regaining its earlier intensity and demonstrating the composure necessary to close out the match. The home players limited Olymp's opportunity to extend the contest, eventually securing the set 25–18 and completing a 3–1 victory. The result confirmed TJ Ostrava's place in the semi-finals and rewarded a performance built around a dominant opening, a brief setback and an effective closing response. For Olymp Praha, the defeat ended the team's cup campaign, despite a spirited effort to recover after falling two sets behind.



The November 18 results added further significance to an increasingly competitive Chance Czech Women's Volleyball Cup. The quarter-final stage had already featured the participation of reigning champions Šelmy Brno, alongside established contenders including Prostějov, Dukla Liberec, Olymp Praha and other leading Czech clubs. With the competition played over single-match quarter-finals, every encounter carried considerable importance, leaving little room for recovery after a disappointing performance. Dukla Liberec and TJ Ostrava can now turn their attention towards the semi-finals, where the standard of opposition is expected to rise further. Their victories underline the importance of consistency, serving pressure, defensive organisation and mental strength in knockout volleyball. As the 2026/27 competition progresses, the remaining contenders will be looking to turn their quarter-final achievements into a place in the final and an opportunity to claim one of the most important domestic honours in Czech women's volleyball.
