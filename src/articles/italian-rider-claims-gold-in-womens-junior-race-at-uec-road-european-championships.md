The race unfolded over a challenging course that tested the young riders with steep climbs and technical descents. Early attacks came from several nations, but the peloton remained largely controlled until the final kilometers. Italy’s leading contender timed her decisive move perfectly, breaking away on the last climb and holding off all challengers to claim the gold medal.

France and the Netherlands both put in strong performances, with their riders securing silver and bronze respectively. The podium reflected the balance of strength across Europe’s top cycling nations, with each team showing depth and determination throughout the race.

Spectators were treated to an aggressive display of racing, with riders refusing to hold back despite the junior category’s shorter distance compared to the elite events. The closing circuits kept fans on edge as breakaways formed and were reeled in, setting the stage for the final decisive attack.

For many of the competitors, this championship serves as a stepping stone toward elite international racing. With standout performances and resilient riding on display, the Women’s Junior race highlighted the bright future of European cycling and underscored the growing strength of the sport at the youth level.
