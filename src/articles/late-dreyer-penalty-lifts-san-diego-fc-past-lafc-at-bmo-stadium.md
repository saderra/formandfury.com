LAFC returned to MLS regular-season action on Saturday, August 15, looking to continue its strong run of form against Southern California rival San Diego FC at BMO Stadium. The Black & Gold entered the evening tied atop the Western Conference with 34 points and carrying an eight-match unbeaten streak across all competitions, while San Diego arrived in Los Angeles searching for an important result to climb the standings.

The match developed into a tightly contested battle in which clear scoring opportunities were difficult to produce. San Diego controlled more of the possession, but LAFC remained dangerous when moving forward, attempting to use the pace and movement of Denis Bouanga and Son Heung-min to create openings against an organized visiting defense.

LAFC also worked to apply pressure through set pieces, finishing the match with nine corner kicks compared with seven for San Diego. Despite those opportunities, the hosts were unable to turn their territorial pressure into a breakthrough as San Diego goalkeeper CJ dos Santos and the defense in front of him repeatedly kept the Black & Gold off the scoreboard.

San Diego struggled to seriously test LAFC goalkeeper Hugo Lloris for much of the night. Through the first 87 minutes, the visitors had managed only one shot on target, a long-range attempt early in the second half that Lloris handled comfortably. That defensive performance appeared to have LAFC positioned to earn at least a point as the match approached its final minutes.

Everything changed following a San Diego corner in the 88th minute. LAFC defender Ryan Porteous was called for a handball near goal, giving San Diego a late penalty and an opportunity to steal all three points. Anders Dreyer stepped forward to take the kick with the match hanging in the balance.

Dreyer made no mistake from the penalty spot, beating Lloris to put San Diego ahead 1-0 in the 89th minute. The goal dramatically changed a match that had seemed destined to finish scoreless and forced LAFC to throw players forward during stoppage time in search of an equalizer.

The closing minutes became even more tense when Dreyer was shown his second yellow card and sent off deep into stoppage time. San Diego was forced to finish the match with 10 players, but LAFC could not take advantage of the brief numerical advantage before the final whistle brought an end to the contest.

Statistically, the matchup reflected how closely the two teams competed. San Diego held 60 percent of the possession and completed 88 percent of its passes, while LAFC recorded three shots on target compared with two for the visitors. LAFC also created the game’s lone officially recorded big chance, but its inability to finish ultimately left the door open for San Diego’s late winner.

The 1-0 defeat ended LAFC’s eight-match unbeaten streak across all competitions and left the club on 34 points, dropping it to third place in the Western Conference following the weekend’s action. San Diego improved to 24 points with the victory, earning valuable ground in the standings and Southern California bragging rights through a dramatic late penalty at BMO Stadium.
