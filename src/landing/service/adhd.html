What You Get

A full evaluation, not a screening

Four components make up the assessment. Together they meet accepted diagnostic standards, which is why the resulting report carries weight with schools, employers, and prescribing providers.

💬

Clinical Interview

A structured diagnostic interview covering developmental history, school and work history, sleep, mood, anxiety, and how attention actually shows up day to day

💻

Conners CPT

A computer-administered measure of sustained attention, impulsivity, vigilance, and reaction time consistency, producing objective performance data rather than self-report

📋

Standardized Rater Scales

Normed forms completed by you and, where appropriate, a parent, teacher, partner, or close friend. Cross-informant ratings are a core part of accepted diagnostic practice

📁

Records Review

Report cards, standardized test scores, prior testing, IEP or 504 documentation, and teacher notes, reviewed and incorporated into the findings

ADHD is not the only condition that makes it hard to focus. Anxiety, sleep problems, learning disorders, depression, and thyroid issues can all produce the same complaint. A careful interview is what separates a real diagnosis from a guess. Ruling things out matters as much as ruling things in.

Your Report

Documentation the right people will accept

You receive a complete written evaluation documenting the referral question, the measures administered, your scores with normative comparisons, diagnostic findings under current DSM criteria, and specific recommendations. It is written to be usable by the people who will read it.

  • ✓ Orange County school districts, for 504 plans and IEP consideration
  • ✓ Colleges and universities, for disability services registration and classroom accommodations
  • ✓ Standardized testing boards, for extended time and other testing accommodations
  • ✓ Employers and HR, for workplace accommodation requests under the ADA
  • ✓ Prescribing physicians and psychiatric providers, who often require documented testing before starting treatment
  • ✓ You, so that a pattern you have lived with for years finally has a name and a plan attached to it

A word of honesty about what a report can and cannot do. Thorough testing gives you strong, well-supported documentation. It does not obligate a school or employer to grant a specific accommodation, and no ethical provider can promise you a particular diagnosis before the evaluation is complete. What I can promise is real testing and a report that holds up.

Why It Matters

Who signs the report changes what it is worth

What a psychologist brings to ADHD testing

Psychological and diagnostic evaluation sits squarely inside a psychologist's scope of practice. That means years of supervised training in test administration, normative interpretation, and differential diagnosis, which is the part that matters most here. Attention problems have a long list of possible causes, and telling them apart is the whole job.

It also means the person reviewing your documentation, whether that is a university disability office, a testing board, or a prescriber, sees a credential they recognize. I conduct and sign every evaluation myself. Nothing is handed off to a technician.

No six-month waitlist

Assessment appointments in Southern California routinely book out half a year, and many practices only test children. Adults, teens, and kids are all seen here, usually within weeks rather than seasons.

Is remote testing as good as in-office?

For ADHD assessment, yes. The measures are the same, the norms are the same, and the interpretation is the same. Children often test better in a familiar room than in a strange building, and parents skip the drive and the missed school day. Choose whichever setting fits your family.

Common Questions

Before you book

Is $1,250 really the whole cost?

Yes. It covers the intake paperwork, the rating scales, the clinical interview, the Conners CPT, the records review, all scoring and interpretation, the written report, and the call where we go through the findings together. There is no hourly billing, no per-test charge, and no separate fee for the report. If the evaluation takes longer than expected, the price does not move.

Do you test adults, or only children?

Both. Children, teens, and adults all go through the same rigorous process, adapted to age. For adults, we cover your history from early childhood forward, since the diagnostic criteria require evidence that symptoms were present early.

Will my child's Orange County school accept this report?

The report is written to support 504 plan and IEP consideration, and it documents everything a district team needs: measures administered, scores with normative comparisons, diagnostic findings, and specific recommendations. Districts make their own eligibility determinations, so no provider can guarantee a particular outcome, but thorough testing gives you the strongest documentation available.

Can you prescribe medication?

No. Psychologists in California do not prescribe. What the evaluation gives you is the documented testing that prescribing physicians and psychiatric providers frequently require before they will consider starting treatment, along with a clear picture of what is driving the symptoms.

What if the testing shows I do not have ADHD?

That happens, and it is a useful result rather than a wasted one. Anxiety, sleep problems, learning disorders, depression, and several medical conditions produce attention complaints that look a great deal like ADHD. Knowing which one you are dealing with is what makes the next step work.

Do I need to bring anything?

Upload whatever records you have: report cards, standardized test scores, prior testing, IEP or 504 documentation, teacher notes, and previous provider records. For children this often carries real weight, since a paper trail going back several years shows patterns no single appointment can.
