Who This Is For

Does any of this sound familiar?

People come to gender-focused therapy from very different places. A few of the ones I hear most often:

  • There is something about your gender you have never said out loud, and the weight of not saying it is getting heavier.
  • You have known since childhood, or the clarity arrived recently and surprised you. Both are real, and both belong here.
  • You are weighing steps, a name, pronouns, how you present, possibly medical care, and the stakes feel enormous in every direction.
  • You transitioned years ago, and there is still work to do: family, dating, grief for lost time, or dysphoria that didn't fully resolve.
  • Your family or faith community is struggling, or you expect them to, and you're carrying their reaction before it has even happened.
  • Your child or teen has told you something about their gender, and you want to respond well without yet knowing what responding well looks like.
  • You're a parent somewhere between fear, love, and a search bar at midnight, and you want guidance from someone with no agenda for your kid.
  • You honestly don't know whether what you feel is gender dysphoria, and you want to sort it out with someone who won't push you toward an answer.

If any of that lands, you're in the right place. None of it is too early, too late, or too uncertain to bring to a first call.

Why It Matters

Why see a specialist for this, specifically

What a psychologist brings to gender dysphoria work

Plenty of therapists list gender identity among a dozen specialties, and no one verifies it. This work asks more than good intentions. It asks for real familiarity with how dysphoria presents across a lifetime, and for the diagnostic training to tell it apart from the depression, anxiety, and trauma that so often arrive with it, or masquerade as it.

My doctoral training covered assessment, diagnosis, and evidence-based treatment, and my clinical background includes work at an LGBT center. You get both: a psychologist's rigor and a therapist who has done this exact work with this community, not adjacent to it.

No educating your own therapist

Session time is expensive, and you should spend none of it defining terms, justifying yourself, or bracing for a bad reaction. We start at the part that's yours.

Is online therapy as good as in person?

For most people, yes. The research on telehealth therapy outcomes is strong, and the conversations are the same ones. Some people speak more freely from their own space, especially about something this personal. Choose whichever setting works, and switch if your needs change.

Common Questions

Before you book

Do I have to want to transition to work with you?

No. Some clients pursue transition, some decide against it, some land somewhere in between, and many arrive with no idea yet. All of those are legitimate places to start and legitimate places to end up. The work is figuring out what is true for you, not arriving at a particular answer.

Do you work with children and teens, or only adults?

All three, with the process adapted to age. For minors, a parent or guardian consents to treatment and stays involved as a partner in the work. Teens also get room to speak freely, with the boundaries of that privacy agreed on together at the start, so nobody is surprised later. The same ground rules apply at every age: patience, no steering, and no conversion therapy in any form.

Will you push me toward, or away from, transition?

Neither. I don't have a script for your outcome. My role is to help you see your own experience clearly, think through decisions with full information, and treat whatever else is going on alongside the gender questions. I do not practice conversion therapy in any form.

What if I'm not sure it's gender dysphoria at all?

Then you are describing one of the most common starting points there is. Dysphoria overlaps with depression, anxiety, trauma responses, and ordinary self-doubt, and untangling them is careful clinical work. That untangling is a core part of what I do, and an uncertain answer at the start is not a problem.

Do you write letters supporting gender-affirming medical care?

Bring this up on the consultation call. Whether documentation makes sense depends on your situation and what a given provider requires, and I will be straightforward with you about what I can and cannot provide before you commit to anything.

I'm not out. Is this safe for me?

Yes. What you share in therapy is confidential within the limits of the law, and I explain those limits plainly in the first session. If privacy at home is a concern for online sessions, mention it on the consultation call and we will work out logistics that protect you.

Do you take insurance?

We go over rates and payment options on the free consultation call, and current details are always on my Rates & Insurance page. Many clients use HSA or FSA funds, and I can provide a superbill for possible out-of-network reimbursement.
