## How Remote ADHD Testing Works

Everything runs through a secure, HIPAA-compliant telehealth platform. You need a computer with a webcam, a stable internet connection, and a quiet space where you will not be interrupted. For children, a parent stays nearby to help with setup and settle the room before testing begins.

**Before your appointment.** I send you secure links to your rating scales and intake paperwork. You complete these online at your own pace, and any observer forms go directly to your rater by email.

**During your appointment.** We meet by video for the clinical interview. When it is time for the performance testing, you launch the Conners CPT on your own machine while we stay connected, so I can confirm conditions and answer questions in real time. Nothing gets mailed, printed, or dropped off.

**After your appointment.** I score and interpret everything, then deliver your written report electronically. We review the findings together on a follow-up video call.

Because I hold a PSYPACT license, I can provide online ADHD testing to residents of more than {{ site.psypactstates }} participating states. If you live somewhere with a six-month waitlist, few qualified evaluators, or a two-hour drive to the nearest testing practice, remote assessment removes that barrier entirely.

Remote testing tends to be easier on families than office testing. Children test in a familiar room instead of a strange building, parents skip the drive and the missed school day, and teacher rating forms arrive by email rather than going home in a backpack.

## What Online ADHD Testing Includes

**Clinical interview.** We start with a structured diagnostic interview by video, covering developmental history, school and work history, sleep, mood, anxiety, and the specific ways attention shows up day to day. For children and teens, I interview parents in depth and speak with the child directly at a level that fits their age. For adults, we cover your own history from early childhood forward, since the criteria require it. ADHD is not the only condition that makes it hard to focus, and a careful interview is what separates a real diagnosis from a guess. Ruling things out matters as much as ruling things in.

**Conners Continuous Performance Test.** The Conners CPT is a computer-administered measure of sustained attention, impulsivity, vigilance, and reaction time consistency. You complete it online during your evaluation, and it produces objective performance data rather than self-report. This is the piece that free screeners and brief primary care visits leave out.

**Standardized rater assessments.** Adults complete normed self-report rating scales through a secure online portal, and where appropriate, a partner, parent, or close friend completes a companion form remotely. For children and teens, parents and teachers complete normed forms describing behavior at home and in the classroom, and older students complete a self-report as well. Cross-informant ratings are a core part of accepted diagnostic practice and carry real weight with schools, employers, and prescribing providers.

**Records review.** Upload report cards, standardized test scores, prior testing, IEP or 504 documentation, teacher notes, or previous provider records, and I review them and incorporate them into the findings. For children this often carries real weight, since a paper trail going back several years shows patterns no single appointment can.

Together these four components make up a full psychological evaluation, not a screening. Remote delivery changes where the testing happens. It does not change what gets measured or how carefully it gets interpreted.

## Your Report

You receive a complete written evaluation report delivered electronically, not a one-page letter. The report documents the referral question, the measures administered, your scores with normative comparisons, diagnostic findings under current DSM criteria, and specific recommendations.

Reports are written to be usable by the people who will read them:

- Schools and universities, for 504 plans, IEP consideration, and disability services registration
- Standardized testing boards, for extended time and other testing accommodations
- Employers and HR, for workplace accommodation requests under the ADA
- Prescribing physicians and psychiatric providers, who often require documented testing before initiating treatment

I am a licensed psychologist, which means your assessment is conducted and signed by a provider whose scope of practice includes psychological and diagnostic evaluation. That distinction matters when an institution reviews your documentation, and it holds whether the testing happened in an office or over video.

A word of honesty about what a report can and cannot do. Thorough testing gives you strong, well-supported documentation. It does not obligate a school or employer to grant a specific accommodation, and no ethical provider can promise you a particular diagnosis before the evaluation is complete. What I can promise is real testing and a report that holds up.
